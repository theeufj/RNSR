## 1. QUALIFICATIONS AND EXPERIENCE

I am a Fellow of the Royal Australasian College of Surgeons (FRACS) and a Fellow of the Australian Orthopaedic Association (FAOrthA). I have practised as a consultant orthopaedic surgeon since 2012. I have a particular interest in trauma surgery and fracture management.

I was the treating surgeon for Ms Mitchell and have seen her on 8 occasions since her admission to Royal Prince Alfred Hospital on 22 January 2025.

## 2. HISTORY OF THE INJURY

Ms Mitchell was involved in a motor vehicle accident on 22 January 2025. She was the driver of a vehicle struck on the driver's side by another vehicle which ran a red traffic light. She was extracted from the vehicle by emergency services after being trapped for approximately 25 minutes.

She was transported to Royal Prince Alfred Hospital Emergency Department, arriving at approximately 9:05 AM.

## 3. INITIAL ASSESSMENT AND DIAGNOSIS

On presentation, Ms Mitchell was alert, oriented, and in significant pain. Initial imaging revealed:

### 3.1 Left Femur Fracture
- **Diagnosis:** Mid-shaft transverse fracture of the left femur
- **Classification:** AO/OTA 32-A3 (simple transverse)
- **Displacement:** Moderate lateral displacement with 15mm shortening
- **Associated injuries:** No vascular or neurological compromise

### 3.2 Rib Fractures
- **Diagnosis:** Fractures of left ribs 5, 6, and 7
- **Location:** Mid-axillary line
- **CT confirmed:** No associated pneumothorax or haemothorax

### 3.3 Left Shoulder
- **MRI (performed 28 January 2025):** Partial-thickness tear of the supraspinatus tendon, approximately 40% thickness, bursal surface
- **No full-thickness component identified**

### 3.4 Cervical Spine
- **CT and MRI:** No bony injury. Soft tissue signal change at C5/6 level consistent with ligamentous strain.

## 4. SURGICAL MANAGEMENT

### 4.1 Femoral Nailing
- **Date:** 23 January 2025
- **Procedure:** Antegrade intramedullary nailing of the left femur
- **Implant:** Synthes Expert Lateral Femoral Nail, 380mm x 11mm, with 2 proximal and 2 distal locking screws
- **Intraoperative findings:** Good reduction achieved. Fracture aligned anatomically. Estimated blood loss 250mL.
- **Post-operative:** Uneventful recovery. Mobilised with physiotherapy on post-operative day 2. Touchdown weight-bearing with crutches for 6 weeks, progressing to partial weight-bearing at 6 weeks and full weight-bearing at 10 weeks.

### 4.2 Rib Fractures
- Managed conservatively with analgesia and incentive spirometry.

### 4.3 Left Shoulder
- Currently managed conservatively with physiotherapy. Surgical intervention (arthroscopic subacromial decompression and rotator cuff repair) may be required if symptoms persist beyond 6 months post-accident.

## 7. PROGNOSIS

### 7.1 Left Femur
- The fracture has united and Ms Mitchell is weight-bearing. However, she continues to experience pain and has a noticeable limp.
- **Further surgery is recommended:** Removal of the intramedullary nail in approximately **12 months** (January 2026). This will require a further general anaesthetic, day surgery, and a recovery period of 4-6 weeks with restricted weight-bearing.
- Even after nail removal, I expect Ms Mitchell will have a **permanent mild limp** and ongoing intermittent pain with prolonged standing or walking.
- There is an **increased risk of early-onset osteoarthritis** in the left hip and knee due to altered biomechanics. This may manifest within 5-10 years.

### 7.2 Left Shoulder
- If conservative management fails, arthroscopic surgery may be required. Recovery from such surgery is typically 3-4 months.
- If surgery is not required, I expect the shoulder to reach maximum medical improvement within 6-9 months, with some residual limitation of overhead activities.

### 7.3 Cervical Spine
- Expected to resolve fully within 6-12 months.
