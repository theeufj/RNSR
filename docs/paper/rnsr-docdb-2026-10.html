RNSR DocDB and Programmable Context Management for Language Models

Joshua Theeuf

21 October 2026 (revised; first version 30 September 2026)

Abstract

Language models that analyze document collections must decide what to bring into context, what to compute outside it, and what evidence to retain for an answer. Increasing the context window does not by itself provide a data model for these decisions. We describe RNSR DocDB, an implemented document environment that stores canonical parsed text, typed relations, search indexes, source coordinates, and versioned semantic annotations in a persistent SQLite artifact. A language-model controller constructs its working context through executable queries, selective reading, and delegated semantic calls. The resulting design separates corpus retention from model visibility, and semantic interpretation from deterministic aggregation. Host-side checks bind quotations and calculations to retained sources, although they do not establish semantic correctness or completeness. We formalize this separation and examine its consequences through an implementation audit, a small matched engineering case study, and a later 286-question run over three synthetic legal matters under a newer model generation, compared against whole-corpus prompting, a flat external-text REPL, and a direct-corpus-interaction baseline. The case study exposes a particularly instructive failure: correct labels and correct arithmetic produced incorrect answers when a repair operation changed the certified population. Two separate trajectories, one month apart and on different model generations, spent their budgets re-submitting drafts to an evidence reviewer rather than converging; the first exhausted the model context despite truncated cell output. On matters of eleven to fifteen thousand tokens, all four arms are statistically indistinguishable on accuracy while DocDB costs twelve to thirty times more; its retrieval layer buys nothing within a context window, and the comparison at a scale where the arms could diverge remains to be run. These findings motivate scope-preserving evidence contracts, explicit review-loop termination, transcript management, and evaluation that distinguishes ingestion, retrieval, interpretation, computation, and operational completion. The contribution is a document-oriented integration and an account of its unresolved systems problems, rather than a claim of benchmark superiority.

1 Introduction

A document question is often a request to construct a view over a collection. The answer may depend on one sentence, every transaction in a period, several versions of a contract, or a classification applied to hundreds of records. These requests impose different requirements on evidence selection. A short passage can settle a sparse lookup, while an exhaustive count requires the intended population to remain intact throughout filtering, interpretation, and aggregation. Treating both requests as the same passage-selection problem conceals this difference.

Retrieval-augmented generation establishes external evidence access as part of language generation [1]. Tool-using agents and external-memory systems make that access iterative [2–4]. RNSR explores a document-specific formulation within this existing design space: a corpus becomes a durable, queryable environment, and the prompt becomes a selected working view of that environment. The model can inspect schemas, run SQL, retrieve passages, read retained text, classify records, and compute answers without copying the full collection into each prompt. Context selection is consequently expressed as a data-access program whose intermediate results can be inspected.

The interesting property of DocDB is the relationship between its representations. Parsed prose remains available when extraction into tables is inadequate. Typed tables support operations over many rows without requiring the controller to reread each cell. Search indexes offer candidate evidence rather than replacing the retained source. Semantic annotations turn expensive model judgments into versioned data that subsequent computations can reuse. Source coordinates connect these views to the text from which they were derived. None of these components is individually new; the research question concerns their composition and the contracts required to make that composition dependable.

This paper makes three contributions. First, it describes a concrete architecture for programmable context management over heterogeneous documents. Second, it separates source identity, computational validity, semantic support, and population completeness, showing why success at one layer does not imply success at another. Third, it analyzes recorded failures of the implemented system and derives a controlled evaluation agenda. The present evidence supports the feasibility and diagnostic value of the design. It does not establish that DocDB improves accuracy, cost, or latency over strong retrieval or long-context alternatives; on the one matched comparison at within-window scale (Section 6.5) it does not.

The implementation examined here is repository revision ded0c42, dated October 2026 [14]. The engineering runs in Sections 6.1–6.3 were recorded against the earlier revision d622678; Sections 6.4 and 6.5 report later runs against ded0c42 and its successor commit, which adds the baseline arms and changes no answering code. Where the two revisions differ in a way that matters to a claim, the text says which revision it describes. Mechanisms described as implemented refer to these revisions. Explicitly proposed contracts and experiments are future work. This distinction matters because parts of the design documentation describe aspirations that exceed the behavior of the current code.

2 Context Management as a Data Access Problem

Three forms of state

DocDB separates persistent corpus state, query-local execution state, and the controller transcript. Let A denote the corpus artifact, with retained parsed documents D, typed relations R, indexes I, manifest M, provenance P, and versioned semantic annotations V. The artifact is persistent across questions. Let S denote variables and intermediate objects in one query's Python environment, and H denote the language-model conversation for that query. These states have different lifetimes and should not be treated as interchangeable memory.

A = ( D, R, I, M, P, V )

Given a question q, the controller selects a program p using the manifest and its prior observations. Execution reads the corpus and query-local state, may request brokered semantic operations, and produces an observation o. Captured cell output is truncated before it enters the controller history; additional harness feedback can follow. Semantic writes use a separate parent-mediated publication path. In the transition below, f denotes that feedback.

p = policy ( q, M, H )
( S′, o ) = execute ( p, A, S )
H′ = append ( H, p, truncate ( o, b ), f )

This model distinguishes reachability from visibility. Retaining a page in D makes it reachable through an appropriate operation; it does not imply that the controller has read it or recognized its relevance. Likewise, retaining a relation permits an exhaustive SQL operation over that relation, but does not imply that it contains all records required by the question. The data-access program is part of the reasoning process and must itself be evaluated.

The current implementation truncates captured cell output to 4,000 characters before appending additional harness feedback, and clips the inline manifest at 20,000 characters. For collections above 100 documents, the manifest substitutes a document count and SQL hint for the document list; table metadata is limited to the first 100 records. These are character and inventory limits, not token guarantees. All previous code and observation turns are retained in the root prompt. Thus bounded observations do not imply bounded total context: history grows with the number and size of steps. DocDB externalizes the corpus, but the present controller still needs a separate policy for managing its accumulated transcript. The revision examined here additionally renders the transcript as an append-only sequence of stable blocks with a provider-side prefix-cache breakpoint on the system prompt and on the last completed turn [16]. For providers that support prefix caching this changes what a growing transcript costs; it does not change what the model must read, and it does not bound transcript length. The 1M-token context windows of the models used in Section 6.4 likewise move the symptom of unbounded growth from a hard context-limit failure to spend, not away.

Why structure changes the available operations

A passage index provides an access path to text. A relational representation additionally exposes operations such as selection, grouping, joins, and aggregation. For a count, the controller can obtain a scalar from a large relation instead of receiving all qualifying rows. For a comparison across periods, it can inspect column names and raw numeric values before computing a difference. For a semantic category, it can materialize per-record decisions and then aggregate those decisions. This changes the units of work available to the agent; it does not remove the need to choose the right units.

The distinction is especially relevant when answer support is dense. A fixed top-k bundle can omit required records whenever the support set is larger than the bundle or distributed across weak lexical matches. An executable program can enumerate a relation or scan retained chunks. Modern retrieval systems can also iterate, aggregate, and use tools, so a fair comparison must isolate the representation and execution policy rather than define all alternatives as one-shot retrieval. The hypothesis is that durable structure makes some useful operations easier to express, reuse, and audit.

3 The DocDB Architecture

Materializing a corpus

Ingestion creates a SQLite artifact containing document metadata, canonical parsed page text, text chunks, extracted tables, and quality metadata. Document records retain source and content hashes, parser identity, and source relationships. Canonical character coordinates are shared by text chunks and quotation checks so that a later reference can resolve against the same retained text. The source binary remains a separate artifact; retaining parsed text is not equivalent to preserving every image, layout feature, or extraction detail of the original document.

Each extracted table becomes a relation with typed columns and row provenance, including page, bounding box when available, extractor, and row kind. Numeric columns retain raw-text shadows alongside coerced values. Total, subtotal, footnote, and section rows can be distinguished from ordinary data rows. This is important because summing a column that contains both line items and a total can be numerically valid yet answer the wrong question. Column coercion and arithmetic consistency checks expose useful signals, but a table marked trusted has passed implemented heuristics rather than a formal extraction proof.

A derived cells relation indexes table text and numeric values without replacing the original typed relations. Text chunks have an FTS5 index. The manifest records schemas, row counts, page ranges, and extraction checks. Ingestion health records track known parse failures, untranscribed scans, and questionable tables; a blocking health state ordinarily prevents answering unless degraded operation is explicitly allowed. This makes some missing evidence visible before retrieval begins, although undetected extraction losses remain possible.

Choosing an access path

The controller operates a persistent Python REPL with read-only access to source data and tools for SQL, search, and selective document reads. A search ladder includes cell matching, regular expressions over retained text, FTS, model-assisted query expansion, optional embedding search, and an explicit semantic sweep over chunks. These paths provide different recall, latency, and cost tradeoffs. The automatic path currently stops at the first nonempty rung, which is a weaker condition than finding sufficient evidence. A controller that needs a complete answer must assess adequacy and may need to request another access path.

An exhaustive sweep means that the system submits retained chunks for semantic examination; it does not guarantee that the model recognizes every relevant passage. Returned hits can also be limited by k. Similarly, SQL exhaustiveness is relative to the selected relations and predicate. Both are useful capabilities, but neither should be described as guaranteed task-level recall. Lazy document loading limits its in-memory cache to 32 documents while leaving stored text reachable. The current sandbox's embedding cache is private and temporary; cross-question embedding reuse is not a general property of this revision.

Materializing semantic work

Some operations cannot be expressed reliably as ordinary predicates over source columns. Determining whether a passage describes a human, whether a clause transfers ownership, or whether a record belongs to a question-specific category requires interpretation. RNSR brokers these judgments through semantic annotation and classification operations. Results are published as annotation columns with a log of the instruction, model identity, selected population, source digests, annotation version, and voting information. Original source columns remain frozen, while annotations can be revised.

Strict classification requires a declared label vocabulary, valid responses for the requested votes, and a strict majority. Voting can reveal unstable decisions, but repeated calls to the same model are correlated judgments, not independent statistical samples. A unanimous label may still be wrong. Materialization therefore changes the visibility and reusability of semantic work rather than turning it into a database fact with guaranteed truth. Source identity and classification policy are part of the cache key: a new document generation or a changed interpretation should invalidate reuse.

A useful workload-level hypothesis follows. If ingestion has cost I and Q questions reuse one artifact, average cost includes I/Q plus the mean query cost. Reused annotations may further reduce repeated interpretation, but only when their scope and semantics remain valid. The opposite outcome is also possible: a persistent incorrect annotation can contaminate many answers. Cold and warm measurements must therefore be separated, and accuracy must be measured alongside any amortization benefit.

Execution and isolation

Generated Python runs in an operating-system-isolated child with private scratch space and scrubbed provider credentials. The parent brokers model calls and permitted annotation writes. This boundary allows the host to re-resolve source references and calculation handles instead of trusting arbitrary values returned by generated code. It is an implementation defense, not a demonstrated security theorem: parent RPC validation, tool design, model prompts, and stored trajectories remain relevant attack surfaces.

The implemented worker calls receive controller-selected prompts; they do not run independent retrieval REPLs. Related questions can share one exploration and return a batch of answers, with callers able to retry missing fields separately. Shared exploration can amortize access but also shares erroneous assumptions and increases transcript growth. In the default research configuration, aggregate query iteration, subcall, wall-time, and spend caps are disabled; a separate production preset sets finite defaults for each. Local execution restrictions, provider limits, and permanent provider errors still apply, and caps bound spend rather than ensure convergence. The examined revision adds one convergence control specific to the evidence reviewer described in Section 4: when a rejected review re-states an objection the reviewer has already adjudicated against unchanged evidence, or when the number of rejected reviews reaches a configured limit, the loop finalizes with the contested fields marked unverified instead of re-submitting the draft. Both thresholds can be set to zero to restore the unbounded loop used in Sections 6.1–6.3.

4 Evidence Contracts and Conditional Correctness

Four distinct questions

An auditable answer must address at least four distinct questions. Does the cited source exist in the retained artifact? Was the computation performed as specified? Do the selected sources support the intended interpretation? Does the selection cover the population required by the question? These properties are related but do not imply one another. A verbatim quotation can concern the wrong year. A correct subtraction can use the wrong financial metric. An exact count can describe only a repaired subset of the required records.

RNSR checks quotation occurrence against normalized retained text and associated coordinates. A separate model-based claim reviewer evaluates selected evidence against the requested claim, with repair feedback and adjudication paths. The reviewer is not given the benchmark gold answer. Its output remains fallible: it can accept an incomplete population, confuse a licence with an ownership transfer, or reject an adequate answer. High, medium, and low evidence tiers are deterministic rules over recorded signals: quotation verification counts, review pushbacks, consensus agreement, negative-answer audit state, ingestion health, the validation status of source tables bound to the answer, and the outcome of claim review. They are not calibrated probabilities of correctness. The table binding is deliberately conservative: a quotation on a page that also holds an unchecked table lowers the tier even when the quotation does not depend on the table. In Section 6.4 this produces high-tier rates between 14% and 50% across otherwise similar matters, which is a calibration cost paid for a rule that can be audited.

Source-bound calculations

When the source-bound numerical path is used, source-number records identify an original numeric cell or a numeric span in retained text, along with source generation and available unit or period context. Parent-side Decimal operations create registered calculation results and their derivations. The finalization path resolves the registered result rather than accepting an unrelated replacement number supplied by the controller. This targets a concrete failure mode in which a model computes from evidence and then substitutes a plausible-looking unsupported answer.

The guarantee is deliberately narrower than numerical answer correctness. Unit and period strings are retained context, not a semantic type system. The host does not thereby prove that operands refer to compatible currencies, the intended year, the requested accounting convention, or the correct entity. General SQL REAL arithmetic also differs from the Decimal calculation path. A complete evidence contract needs both mechanically checkable derivation and an explicit account of why those operands answer the question.

Population identity must survive repair

For a classification count, let U be the population requested by the question, V the population selected by the execution, and L the materialized label function. The program can correctly count records in V with label c while still risking an incorrect answer if V differs from U or L is wrong. Even agreement of the final totals would not establish either scope or label correctness. A certificate that binds a computation to V establishes a useful mechanical property, but the task requires an additional correspondence between the question and that population.

The current classification finalizer revalidates the most recent contract for a table and annotation column. A partial repair can therefore replace the contract for the original population with one for a subset. Section 6.2 documents two answers for which the final labels were correct but the resulting count used the repair population. This is a failure of contract composition: the local repair was meaningful, yet its identity was incorrectly promoted to the identity of the whole task.

A proposed stronger contract would identify the corpus generation, question scope, selected row identities, classification policy, label vocabulary, model configuration, annotation version, and permitted aggregation. Repair operations would publish deltas against that same population. Finalization would check that every required row has a valid current label and that the aggregate uses the original scope. The question-to-population mapping would still require semantic or human evaluation, but a repair could no longer silently redefine it. This contract is a design proposal. It is not implemented in ded0c42: the finalizer still revalidates the newest contract recorded for a table and annotation column, so the failure in Section 6.2 remains reproducible.

5 An Illustrative Document Workflow

Consider a hypothetical collection of invoices, credit notes, and amendments, and the question: What is the net invoiced amount for a particular project in the financial year? The example illustrates the intended decomposition; it is not a reported experimental result. A text-only answer might require repeatedly copying individual amounts into a prompt. DocDB instead makes the controller's choices inspectable at the level of documents, rows, values, and transformations.

The controller first inspects the manifest for candidate tables and searches retained text for project identifiers and document relationships. It must determine which documents are final, which credits reverse earlier invoices, and which dates define the requested period. These are scope and interpretation decisions. A schema match alone is insufficient: two columns called amount may refer to line values and document totals, or to different currencies.

Next, the controller selects the intended records and inspects representative raw values, row kinds, and supporting passages. It can express straightforward filters in SQL and use semantic annotations where document status requires interpretation. For a supported source-bound calculation, it registers the selected amounts and computes a net result through the host. It then supplies the result together with evidence for the population and financial convention. A reviewer can ask for missing amendment text or challenge the treatment of a credit note without requiring the entire corpus in one prompt.

The resulting benefit is an inspectable chain of decisions: evidence discovery, population construction, source-value selection, computation, and answer acceptance. Each link can fail separately. If one scanned invoice was never transcribed, retrieval cannot recover it. If a duplicate invoice is counted twice, arithmetic can remain correct. If the reviewer sees only the selected documents, it may not notice a missing one. The workflow is valuable because it exposes these dependencies and offers places to measure or check them, not because relational execution makes them disappear.

6 Engineering Evidence and Failure Analysis

6.1 Study scope and protocol

The empirical material consists of recorded engineering runs and a read-only audit of their code, configurations, trajectories, and diagnostics [14, 15]. It is not a preregistered benchmark study. The broad September 29 run planned 17 jobs and 524 benchmark executions or local-form fields. It stopped after an Anthropic account usage-limit failure. Because that scope mixes benchmark questions and extracted fields, a single aggregate accuracy would combine incompatible units and obscure missingness. We instead use a small matched control to examine mechanisms and report operational coverage separately.

The matched control contains five locally generated classification-count questions over the same 400-row synthetic source. It is inspired by the semantic-analysis-then-aggregation workload of Oolong [9], but it is not the published Oolong benchmark. The DocDB and classic external-text modes used the same questions, source, gold answers, root model alias claude-sonnet-4-5, and submodel alias claude-haiku-4-5. Scoring used exact or numeric comparison with a shared Haiku equivalence-judgment fallback. Query concurrency was two, maximum provider concurrency six, seed 42, and aggregate query and run spend limits were zero, meaning disabled. Model aliases were recorded rather than immutable provider snapshots. Costs in Table 2 are list-price estimates at the September 2026 rates for those aliases. The scorer used for Table 2 accepted exact normalized text, numeric agreement within one percent, or text containment, with a model judge on disagreement; the scorer used in Section 6.4 is stricter. Table 2 is therefore not re-scored and the two sections are not directly comparable.

This comparison tests the configured systems, not the isolated effect of database structure. In particular, the semantic claim-review path was active only for DocDB. Both modes had scored 5/5 on the same control in the earlier September 28 run at revision 8657dd1. The later code and review configuration changed, so the temporal comparison does not identify which single change caused the regression. Five questions from one shared source also provide very little statistical power or workload diversity.

6.2 Correct labels with the wrong population

Two DocDB failures counted location and abbreviation labels. The required answers were 65 and 55; the returned answers were 6 and 4. The retained final annotations contained correct labels for the full 400-row source. An earlier voting response had omitted one row twice, leaving a 40-row batch unresolved under the strict validity rule. Repairing those rows wrote a newer contract for that 40-row selection. Finalization then counted that selection rather than the complete task population, and the semantic reviewer accepted the narrower result.

This case isolates an important distinction. Improving the classifier would not directly repair these two final answers: the final labels were already correct. The aggregate was also correct for its selected scope. The failed invariant was preservation of task population through a local repair. It provides evidence for strengthening scope identity and certificate composition, rather than treating every missed count as a retrieval or classification-quality problem. It also shows why a semantic reviewer should not be trusted to reconstruct an invariant that the execution layer can preserve explicitly.

6.3 External storage does not control transcript growth

A third synthetic DocDB question returned no answer after 342 iterations, about 31.4 minutes, and $112.76 in recorded API usage. Its final provider request contained 200,101 tokens against a 200,000-token context limit. The trajectory repeatedly pursued certification rather than converging. The corpus had been externalized and captured cell output was truncated, but the accumulated code, observations, and harness feedback were still appended to the next root prompt. This is direct evidence that durable corpus storage and controller-context management are separate systems requirements.

A proposed repair is to checkpoint typed execution state and a compact evidence ledger, while retaining the complete trajectory outside the active prompt. Progress should be defined in terms of newly covered evidence, resolved obligations, or changed hypotheses. Repeated equivalent states should trigger a different access path, a richer worker, or an explicit unresolved outcome. Such policies need controlled evaluation: aggressive summarization can lose constraints, and premature termination can recreate the missed-answer problem they are intended to address.

The circuit breaker described in Section 3 is a response to this trajectory and to the one in Section 6.4. It bounds the number of review rounds, not the transcript. Checkpointing typed execution state and a compact evidence ledger, and defining progress in terms of newly covered evidence, remain unimplemented proposals.

6.4 A second review-loop pathology under a newer model generation

On 20 October 2026, revision ded0c42 was run over three synthetic legal matters authored for benchmarking: an employment dispute (40 documents, 111 questions), a family-law property matter (37 documents, 85 questions), and a partner-visa matter (43 documents, 90 questions). Documents include Word and PDF files, plain-text correspondence, and photographed or scanned pages that required vision-model transcription. Each matter carries a question set with gold answers, a per-question type, and a list of the source documents an answer should rest on. Unanswerable items (false premises, facts absent from the corpus) are included and scored as correct only when the system declines to answer. The matters were not used for tuning, but they were written to exercise known failure modes (superseded facts, distractors, absent premises), so they are diagnostic coverage rather than an independent held-out test.

The matters are small. Retained text is 52,538, 44,381, and 60,054 characters respectively, roughly 11,000 to 15,000 tokens each, with no single document above 2,400 characters. Each matter fits in one model call with a wide margin. These runs therefore sit inside the regime where earlier internal measurements found no accuracy advantage for DocDB over placing the document in context, and they say nothing about collections that exceed a context window. What they test is whether the system completes a multi-document workload on realistic document types, including photographed and scanned pages, at a known cost. Section 6.5 reports the baseline arms that make the comparison explicit.

The configuration was root model claude-sonnet-5-5, sub-model claude-haiku-5-5 for batched sub-calls, claim review, and judging, batches of eight questions per exploration, per-query caps of 16 root iterations, 300 seconds, and $1 (scaled for batch size), and claim review enabled. Scoring used the revision's stricter deterministic scorer: exact normalized text or a scalar whose currency, scale, and unit dimension agree, with no text-containment rule. Disagreements were sent to a model judge that must return a complete YES or NO; an unresolved verdict is a miss.

The first attempt failed operationally. Every matter reached a $60 run spend ceiling with most questions unanswered: 120 final answers for $181.29 across the three matters. Four causes were identified, all specific to the model generation. The models reject non-default sampling temperature; they think adaptively by default and thinking tokens count against the output limit, so terse deterministic calls (an eight-token YES/NO judge, a 64-token recovery call) returned empty text; the claim reviewer's 2,048-token output cap truncated the JSON verdict for batches of eight fields, so 375 of 440 reviews failed to parse, the unresolved review sent the draft back to the controller, and the cycle repeated until the budget was exhausted; and a scan transcription parser rejected a page whose text blocks were returned inside the table array. Individual batches reached 145 root iterations and 10 million input tokens.

This is the same failure shape as Section 6.3 on a different model generation with a different proximate trigger. In September the reviewer kept rejecting; in October the reviewer's reply could not be read. In both cases the loop treated an unresolved review as an instruction to try again with the same evidence, and nothing other than a spend cap stopped it. The circuit breaker in Section 3 was added after this run and was not active during it.

6.5 Baseline arms at the same scale

On 21 October 2026 the same 286 questions were answered by three baselines that differ from the DocDB run only in how the corpus is presented. The retained text, models (claude-sonnet-5-5 root, claude-haiku-5-5 sub-model and judge), per-query caps, batch size, scorer, and gold answers are identical. Whole matter in context places every document in the prompt and makes one call per question, with the corpus as a cached prefix. Classic is the flat external-text environment of Section 6.1: one string in the REPL that the controller slices and regex-searches. Direct corpus interaction writes one text file per document into the sandbox and exposes ls, grep (shaped like rg -n), and read (shaped like sed -n), following [18]; the recorded trajectories show the same pattern that paper reports, broad pattern search followed by narrowing and bounded reads. Neither REPL baseline has typed tables, a search ladder, annotations, quotation verification, or claim review.

The four arms lie within seven questions of one another on 286. The binomial standard error at this accuracy is about three questions, so none is distinguishable from another on accuracy. They are distinguishable on cost, by a factor of twelve to thirty, and on wall time, by four to seven. On the dense question types the relational representation was meant to serve, counts and relationships, DocDB is not ahead: both REPL baselines answered every count question, DocDB missed two. Every baseline miss is one of the two scorer artifacts noted in Section 6.4, a correct answer with added context the judge rejected or a decline to answer where the gold is "No"; eight of the nine classic misses on existence questions are the latter. None is a retrieval failure. The total provider spend for all nine baseline runs and their judging was under seven dollars.

The reading is direct. At eleven to fifteen thousand tokens per matter the corpus fits in one call, and placing it there is the cheapest option and as accurate as any other. DocDB's retrieval layer buys nothing at this scale, and its claim-review loop accounts for most of the cost difference. This is the outcome earlier internal measurements predicted for within-window corpora, now measured under the current scorer against the baselines the evaluation agenda in Section 8 asks for. It is also the outcome the direct-corpus-interaction papers would predict: on a corpus this small, grep sees everything.

What the comparison does not show is equally direct. It does not reach collections that exceed a context window, dense aggregation over hundreds of rows, or questions whose support is spread across many weak lexical matches, which are the conditions under which Section 2 argues structure should matter. And it does not measure the evidence layer: no baseline arm produces source-bound calculations, coordinate-checked quotations, bound table status, or a reviewed claim, and the scorer does not reward them. On this workload the paper's defensible contribution is therefore the evidence contracts and the failure analysis, not retrieval. Whether the retrieval layer earns its cost at a scale where the arms can diverge is the matched experiment that remains to be run.

6.6 Operational and evaluation failures

The broader September 29 run saved 171 outcome records out of 524 planned units before stopping. Nine records contained returned answers, of which seven were judged correct and two incorrect. The remaining 162 records were execution errors; 113 carried Boolean failure scores and 49 local-form fields were left unscored. Another 353 planned units had no saved result. The 113 failure scores should count against operational success when that metric is reported, but they should not be described as 113 semantically wrong model answers. Recorded usage for the run was $275.17 and may omit charges for interrupted work.

Quota failures account for much of this missingness and make the run unsuitable for a headline accuracy claim. Other diagnostics found validation and harness defects: task boilerplate falsely triggered a classification-proof requirement on ordinary form questions, a scalar response reached code expecting a batch object, and duplicated question context invalidated local-field scoring. The local-matter judging protocol had also changed between runs. These observations are useful engineering evidence, but they do not isolate retrieval quality. Private matter names and source passages are omitted here; local fields are not used to claim public benchmark performance. The October attempt in Section 6.4 belongs in the same category: an operational failure whose 120 answers should not be aggregated with the 286 of the rerun.

7 Related Work

External evidence and managed memory. RAG combines a parametric generator with retrieval from nonparametric memory [1]. ReAct interleaves reasoning and actions [2], and CodeAct uses executable code as an action representation [12]. MemGPT explicitly manages movement between in-context and external memory tiers [4]. Recursive Language Models are the closest conceptual predecessor: they expose the input as an object in an external REPL and let the model inspect, decompose, and process it with submodel calls [3]. RNSR's specific emphasis is a persistent document representation with relational structure and source coordinates. It does not introduce external memory, tool use, or recursive model calls. Scroll [17] treats an agent's own session history the same way: an append-only event log with stable addresses, a persistent typed Python namespace, and a working view assembled from printed projections, with evicted spans recoverable by address. That is a worked implementation of the transcript policy Section 6.3 proposes and does not implement; its evaluations are on conversational-memory and long-horizon agent benchmarks, not document corpora, so its results do not transfer to DocDB and DocDB's do not bear on it.

Direct corpus interaction. A 2026 line of work drops the retriever entirely and lets an agent search raw document files with shell tools: grep, file reads, and small scripts [18]. On BRIGHT, BEIR subsets, and BrowseComp-Plus this outperforms sparse, dense, and reranked retrieval, and the authors' trace analysis finds the agent spending most of its actions on bounded pattern searches rather than reading. Follow-up work bounds the interaction space with a retriever because raw shell search degrades past roughly 105 documents [19, 20]. The first three rungs of RNSR's search ladder are this interface expressed in Python; Section 6.5 reports a direct-corpus-interaction baseline built that way. The open question those papers leave, and the one RNSR's typed relations are meant to answer, is whether structure buys anything over grep when the answer is an aggregate rather than a passage.

Semantic data processing. PAL delegates computation to a program runtime [5]. Binder embeds model calls within symbolic programs [11], while SUQL composes SQL with operations over free text [10]. LOTUS provides relational semantic operators and optimizations with guarantees relative to reference algorithms [6]. These systems are direct precedents for separating interpretation from execution. RNSR's source-bound calculation and answer-acceptance paths ask a narrower document question: which retained evidence and population justify a computed result? The statistical guarantees of LOTUS do not transfer to RNSR's voting or review mechanisms.

Provenance and long-context evaluation. Provenance semirings formalize how relational outputs depend on input tuples [7]. RNSR records source identities, coordinates, votes, and operation traces, but does not implement that provenance algebra. Lost in the Middle shows that the tested models' effective use of evidence depends on its position, motivating evaluation beyond advertised context capacity [8]. Oolong emphasizes semantic reasoning across many items followed by aggregation [9], a useful distinction from sparse retrieval. Neither result establishes that DocDB solves those workloads. Recursive Agent Harnesses study giving delegated workers full agent capabilities [13]; that direction is relevant to richer document workers, but their reported gains do not establish gains for RNSR.

8 An Evaluation Agenda

Isolate representation and policy

A decisive evaluation should hold source documents, questions, model snapshots, prompts where applicable, provider concurrency, and scoring constant, while varying one architectural component at a time. Useful variants include a flat external-text environment, text plus FTS, typed source tables, semantic materialization, source-bound calculations, and semantic claim review. Baselines should include both fixed retrieval and strong iterative retrieval with tools, as well as direct long-context input where feasible. Comparing only against a deliberately restricted retrieval bundle would not identify the value of DocDB. Section 6.5 runs three of these variants at a scale where every arm sees the whole corpus; the same arms must be run where they cannot.

The workload matrix should vary collection size, schema heterogeneity, answer-support cardinality, question reuse, and evidence dispersion independently. Sparse lookup, dense counting, joins across documents, version-sensitive interpretation, and arithmetic require separate analysis. Source corruption, duplicated totals, missing scans, ambiguous labels, subset repair, and provider interruption should be explicit fault-injection conditions. These tests examine the system's contracts rather than merely repeating prompts that resemble previous failures.

Measure the chain of evidence

Metrics should distinguish source availability after ingestion, retrieval of required evidence, item-level semantic accuracy, task-population coverage, arithmetic validity, final answer accuracy, and operational completion. For aggregate questions, both exact counts and underlying row-level errors are needed: false positives and false negatives can cancel in one total while remaining semantically wrong. For reviewed answers, measure false acceptance and false rejection against independently adjudicated examples. Evidence tiers require calibration before they can be interpreted as confidence estimates.

Cost and latency should include ingestion, annotation construction, successful answering, repair, failed attempts, and judging. Report cold and warm artifacts separately, together with context tokens by iteration, repeated-state counts, and latency and spend distributions that expose long tails. Censored runs must retain their execution errors and missing coverage. Paired comparisons should use shared question identities and the same scoring protocol; heterogeneous fields and benchmark questions should not be collapsed into one percentage. Sample size and uncertainty should accompany any aggregate result.

Two further measurements follow from Section 6.4. A review-loop termination policy needs its own evaluation: the rate at which a circuit breaker halts a loop that would have converged, against the budget it saves when the loop would not. And when a model judge decides most correct verdicts, the judge is part of the system under test; report the judge-scored share, retain judge explanations, and adjudicate a sample of judge decisions independently before treating an accuracy figure as a property of the answering system alone.

Preserve a held-out test

Development can use diagnosed failures, but adoption decisions require unseen controls. Freeze the interpretation policy and test set before measuring a change. Keep disputed references separate from confirmed system errors, retain judge explanations, and independently review disagreement cases and a sample of unanimous decisions. An experiment that selects only repaired historical misses can establish that those cases changed; it cannot establish general improvement. The five-question control in Section 6 is diagnostic evidence and should not be used as the validation set for the proposed scope fix.

9 Limitations and Responsible Use

DocDB shifts complexity into ingestion, schema construction, state management, and execution policy. Tables can be malformed, source passages can be missing, annotations can be stale, and the controller can select an inappropriate predicate. Persistence makes reuse possible and also preserves mistakes. The present transcript policy still permits unbounded growth; the review circuit breaker bounds one cause of repetition, not transcript size. Richer workers and hierarchical routing remain proposed extensions. SQLite is a practical implementation choice, but no large-scale capacity or speed claim follows from choosing it. A new storage engine would require measured bottlenecks before it could be justified.

Source-linked answers remain inappropriate for automatic high-stakes reliance without domain review. A supported quotation may still be insufficient for a legal or financial conclusion. Prompt injection in source documents can influence the model even when generated code is contained. Provider calls and detailed trajectories may expose sensitive passages, so deployment needs explicit data-access policy, retention controls, and an understanding of provider handling. The anonymized local examples in this paper do not constitute a privacy or security evaluation.

The current evidence is limited by small matched controls, changing engineering configurations, provider failures, mutable model aliases, and selected historical coverage. No comparison with published state-of-the-art scores is made. The paper's contribution is a concrete systems design and an analysis of where its assumptions fail. Demonstrating an accuracy or efficiency advantage requires the controlled experiments described above. The October runs add their own limits: a stricter scorer that routes most prose answers to a model judge and whose rejections account for every baseline miss, cost estimates that under-count tiered sub-model pricing, matters whose authors knew the system, and corpora small enough that every arm sees all of them. Provider behaviour also changed under the paper's feet: sampling, thinking, and output-limit semantics differed between the model generations used in Sections 6.1–6.3 and 6.4, and each difference produced an operational failure before it produced a measurement.

10 Conclusion

RNSR DocDB treats document context as queryable state that a language-model controller can inspect and transform. Retained prose, typed relations, indexes, and semantic annotations provide complementary ways to construct working context, while source-bound execution makes parts of an answer independently checkable. The central engineering lesson is that these checks compose only when source identity, task population, and interpretation remain aligned. Correct labels and arithmetic can still yield a wrong answer; external storage can still coexist with an overflowing prompt; an evidence reviewer can turn a stall into a spend; and within a context window, a relational corpus answers no better than the corpus itself at many times the cost. A dependable implementation therefore needs explicit scope contracts, review-loop termination, transcript management, and evaluation across the full chain from source ingestion to accepted answer.
