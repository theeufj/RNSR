One-line summary: within a context window every architecture ties on accuracy. DocDB's measurable advantages are (1) it reads documents the others cannot (scans), (2) it answers aggregation questions beyond the context window where prompting collapses, and (3) it is the only arm that produces a verified citation on every answer and a replayable derivation on computed ones. It costs 2–3× a plain REPL for that. Those three claims are now measured; the trust-tier layer still is not a signal.

What we built

The system in one picture

Ingest → corpus artifact

  • Docling layout parse, OCR/transcription of scanned pages, typed tables with character coordinates, FTS index, health record — one SQLite file per matter.
  • Frozen: every answer binds to row ids and spans a human can re-open.

Controller in a sandboxed REPL

  • Sonnet 5.5 root, Haiku 5.5 sub-calls; SQL, search ladder (FTS → embeddings), semantic_annotate / semantic_classify, source-bound calculate and aggregate.
  • Budgets: 24 root iterations, 600 s, $2 per query; run spend ceiling.

Evidence layer

  • Quotation verification, parent-executed certificates (the parent re-runs the selection SQL and re-reads every row), claim reviewer, trust tiers.

Evaluation harness (the part that grew most this month)

  • Five arms over identical corpora: whole-document prompting, classic REPL, DCI (files + grep), DCI over raw files, DocDB; plus a closed-book arm (no documents).
  • One-command matter bench; three public benchmarks; repeats; Wilson intervals and paired McNemar tests; two-provider judge with hand adjudication file; gold overlay with cited reasons.
  • Diagnostics: auditability counters (verified citation, replayable derivation), OOLONG per-line label accuracy against gold labels.
Design stance: the paper and the code must agree. Every number in the paper is produced by a command in the repository and pinned to a commit.

Assessment

Is it valuable? What the evidence supports, and what it does not

Supported by measurement

  • Beyond the context window, a REPL (any REPL) beats prompting on aggregation 15/15 vs 3–4/15. That part is commodity: DCI and classic get it too.
  • Layout-aware ingestion is worth ~18% of questions on a corpus with scans, and 0% on clean PDFs.
  • DocDB is the only arm with a verified citation on every answer and a replayable derivation on 54–76% of computed ones. This is the product property; no baseline has it.
  • Cost of that property: 2–3× a REPL and ~3× its wall time, down from ~10× in September.

Not supported (yet)

  • No accuracy advantage over a plain REPL anywhere. Claims of "more accurate" should not be made.
  • Trust tiers do not predict correctness: on the dense matter 22/38 correct answers are low-tier because the reviewer cannot check scope.
  • Half of FinanceBench is in the model's memory; the benchmark measures recall as much as reading. Any public-10-K result needs the closed-book baseline beside it.
  • Judge choice moves scores by 5–12 questions on 40. Differences of a few questions are not findings.
So what: the pitch is auditability at a known price, not accuracy. The relational layer is what makes a derivation replayable; the question for legal-tech is whether a receipt a reader can re-open is worth 2–3× the inference cost. That is now a priced, product question rather than a research one.
