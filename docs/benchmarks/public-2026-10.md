# Public benchmarks, five arms — October 2026

Run date: 2026-10-22, revision `3d62d4b` (DocDB FinanceBench arm) and `309c2fd` (all other arms). The same layer-by-layer protocol as the generated matters ([matters-2026-10.md](matters-2026-10.md)), applied to three public benchmarks through the eval harness: identical models (`claude-sonnet-5-5` root, `claude-haiku-5-5` sub-calls, review and judge), identical caps (24 root iterations, 600 s, $2 per query, $40 run ceiling), identical scorer (v3 strict scalar/text plus a model judge whose unresolved verdict is a miss), and a shared corpus cache so every arm reads the same ingested artifact.

> These figures supersede the July–September README results archived in [benchmark-history.md](benchmark-history.md), which were scored by text containment and first-number matching and are not comparable. Where it matters, the July *predictions* are rescored under today's scorer below so that a change in the ruler can be told apart from a change in the system.

## Arms

| Arm | Corpus presentation |
|---|---|
| Whole document in context | every document in the prompt, one call per question, cached prefix |
| Classic | ingested text as one string in the REPL |
| DCI | ingested text, one file per document, `ls`/`grep`/`read` in the REPL |
| DCI over raw files | fast text extraction of the original files: no layout model, no tables, no transcription |
| DocDB | typed tables, search ladder, annotations, certificates, claim review, trust tiers |

Whole-document, classic and DCI arms have no quotation verification, review or tiers. Spend is list-price provider usage summed over the questions; judge cost is excluded. Judge-scored is the number of correct verdicts decided by the model judge rather than exact match.

## FinanceBench, stratified 40-question subset

Real SEC filings: 18 10-Ks/10-Qs, median 557k characters of retained text (284k–912k), median 97 Docling-extracted tables per filing (max 175). The subset is seeded (seed 11), balanced 20 numeric / 20 textual, concentrated on filings with several questions; the qid list is [financebench-subset-2026-10.json](financebench-subset-2026-10.json) so the run is a strict subset of any later full run. Corpora are the July Docling parses carried forward with `rnsr.eval.migrate_cache` (`cells` and `ingest_batches` rebuilt, `validate_frozen` passing); no filing was re-parsed.

| Arm | Correct | Accuracy | Judge-scored | Spend | Latency p50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole document in context | 24 / 40 | 60.0% | 24 | $9.24 | 7 s |
| Classic | 24 / 40 | 60.0% | 17 | $0.86 | 11 s |
| DCI | 22 / 40 | 55.0% | 13 | $1.15 | 13 s |
| DCI over raw files | 26 / 40 | 65.0% | 19 | $0.94 | 12 s |
| DocDB | 25 / 40 | 62.5% | 25 | $3.00 | 25 s |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `numeric` (20) | 14 | 14 | 13 | 13 | 11 |
| `textual` (20) | 10 | 10 | 9 | 13 | 14 |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Verified citation | 0 / 40 | 0 / 40 | 0 / 40 | 0 / 40 | 40 / 40 |

| Tiers H/M/L | – | – | – | – | 0 / 40 / 0 |

Reading. Five arms within 22–26 of 40, which on n=40 is one standard error; no ordering is reliable. Of 24 questions missed by any arm, 7 are missed by all five, and those are gold-format mismatches rather than wrong answers: gold `$382.00` against "$381.6 million"; gold `0.01` (a ratio) against "1.42%"; gold `77.78` for a question every arm read as asking for $700M; a Boeing effective tax rate whose gold carries the opposite sign convention to the filing's own table. Every 10-K fits a 1M-token window, so whole-document prompting ties the REPL arms at ten times their cost. DCI over raw files is not worse here, unlike on the heterogeneous dense matter, because 10-K PDFs have clean text layers and there are no scans to lose: ingestion's measured value is in unreadable pages, not in PDFs that already read. DocDB is the only arm with a parent-verified citation on every answer and a replayable derivation on 12 of 20 numeric questions, at three to four times the REPL arms' cost; the embedding gate held (0 batches) and no batch halted.

**Did DocDB get worse since July?** Partly, and the part is identifiable. July's own scorer reported 39/40 on these questions. July's *predictions*, rescored under today's scorer and judge, score **29/40**; today's DocDB scores **24–25/40** (two judge passes). So ten of the fifteen lost points are the ruler and four to five are the system. The seven questions that flipped all have the right number in today's answer and differ in presentation: July answered `0.01`, `101.5%`, `7.9%`, `1615.9`; today's certificate path answered "ROA = 1.42% (net income … divided by …)", "…is 101.5 percent, computed as…", a raw 50-decimal quotient for a quick ratio, and "$1,615.9 million, per the Consolidated Balance Sheet". A fix landed mid-run (`present()` bounds a `FINAL_CALC` answer to four decimals; the prompt asks for the number first in the form the question implies) and the DocDB arm was rerun: it recovered one question and raised replayable derivations from 10 to 12 of 20, but the judge still rejects "7.9%, computed as the mean of…" against a gold of `7.9%` and "101.5%, computed as…" against `101.5%`. That is the judge, not the system; on this benchmark judge inconsistency on verbose-but-correct answers is the dominant noise, and it applies to every arm (whole-document prompting's 24 correct verdicts were all judge decisions).

Misses by arm are in `runs/public-arms/financebench-report.md`; the pre-fix DocDB run is preserved at `runs/public-arms/_prev-financebench-docdb-presentation/`.

## CUAD-long, 10 contracts

Real contracts, 200k–750k characters each, prose with no tables; 7 absent-clause and 3 extraction questions. Corpora ingested fresh (Docling).

| Arm | Correct | Spend | Latency p50 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole document in context | 7 / 10 | $0.57 | 6 s |
| Classic | 7 / 10 | $0.55 | 33 s |
| DocDB | 6 / 10 | $1.36 | 72 s |

Three of the four missed questions are missed by all three arms. On n=10 nothing is distinguishable; the within-window finding holds on real contracts. DocDB's structure has nothing to work with here and costs 2.5× for it.

## OOLONG, 50 questions (trec_coarse, 1k–65k tokens)

Flat synthetic text; 41 classify-then-count questions and 9 per-item questions. This is the classification-count path (`semantic_classify` with votes, parent `classification_aggregate`), not the certificate.

| Arm | Correct | Spend | Latency p50 | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole document in context | 33 / 50 | $0.99 | 5 s | 50 final |
| Classic | 38 / 50 | $1.92 | 34 s | 50 final |
| DocDB | 33 / 50 | $16.77 | 44 s | 48 final, 2 budget_exhausted |

DocDB: 453 root calls, 411 classification sub-batches, 113 reviews, 11 halts, tiers 0 high / 37 medium / 13 low. Eight of 24 missed questions are missed by all three arms. July's archived figures were DocDB 64% and classic 60%/58% under the loose scorer; DocDB is where it was and classic improved with the newer model. The classification-vote path is the most expensive thing in the system, 8–17× the other arms here, and no more accurate than the controller labelling items itself with `llm_map`. On a benchmark that fits in a prompt, the prompt is as good as the certified count and seventeen times cheaper.

## What the public benchmarks add

They confirm the generated-matter decomposition rather than change it:

- Within a context window, the arms tie on accuracy on real documents too (FinanceBench, CUAD-long); whole-document prompting costs 10× the REPL arms on 10-Ks and about the same on contracts.
- Ingestion's value is in documents a model cannot otherwise read. Clean-text 10-Ks and contracts give it nothing to recover, and the raw-files arm is at parity; on the heterogeneous dense matter with 34 scans it was worth seven questions.
- DocDB's distinct output is still the receipt: a verified citation on every FinanceBench answer and a replayable derivation on 12 of 20 numeric questions, at 3–4× the cost. No other arm produces either.
- The classification-count path (OOLONG) is DocDB's weakest layer on cost and buys nothing on accuracy.
- The strict scorer plus a single-pass model judge is the limiting instrument on FinanceBench: it penalises every arm for stating a correct number with its formula, and it mis-scores golds whose format the question does not imply. Two judge passes and a judge rubric that treats "X%, computed as …" as equal to "X%" are the next evaluation changes, ahead of any system change.

## Reproduce

```bash
export RNSR_MAX_ROOT_ITERS=24 RNSR_MAX_WALL_S=600 RNSR_MAX_SPEND_USD=2 RNSR_RUN_SPEND_CEILING_USD=40
python -m rnsr.eval.migrate_cache --from runs/financebench/financebench-docdb/corpora --to runs/public-arms/corpora   # or let the harness re-ingest
for s in stuffing rlm-classic dci dci-raw docdb; do
  rnsr eval -b financebench -s $s --stratify 40 --seed 11 --run-dir runs/public-arms --corpus-cache runs/public-arms/corpora -c 3
done
python -m rnsr.eval.public_arms_report --run-dir runs/public-arms --benchmark financebench
for b in "cuad-long 10" "oolong 50"; do set -- $b
  for s in stuffing rlm-classic docdb; do rnsr eval -b $1 -s $s -n $2 --run-dir runs/public-arms --corpus-cache runs/public-arms/corpora-$1 -c 3; done
  python -m rnsr.eval.public_arms_report --run-dir runs/public-arms --benchmark $1 --systems stuffing,rlm-classic,docdb
done
```
